A. Top Recommendation

PASS

Even though they did find a relevant issue, especially for the Polish market (their main and so far only market), the product itself does not contain any technological know-how, nor do they seem to have any point that will tie clients to them. The product is relatively easily replicable; any tech-savvy user will manage to build the same thing just for their system using APIs in a weekend. Also, the system at this point is pretty much a complicated reminder feature, with good automations. So far, their main differentiator is focus on the Polish market and Poland-specific tooling, but moving down this road with the current direction, they won't be big enough to cover Fund's Risks.

Sources & Footnotes
  • No technical moat: All 6 Polish accounting systems Sunbay integrates with expose public REST APIs rated Low to Moderate integration difficulty. "The integration layer Sunbay has built is technically replicable by any competitor -- there is no proprietary connector advantage." (competitor_addition.md)
  • Product scope: Core workflow is "connect accounting system -> define collection rules and templates -> system sends reminders on schedule -> dashboard tracks status." Confirmed shipped features are limited to reminder sequences, workflows, tracking, interest notes, and dashboard. (product_analysis.md)
  • Polish-only advantage: No international player (Chaser, Upflow, Gaviti, Tesorio) has any Polish integrations. (direct_competitor_landscape.md, Part C)
  • Fund math: $1.2M check on $70M fund (eu.vc confirmed) = ~1.7% of Fund II capital. At 20% ownership, required exit for 1x fund return = $350M (pre-dilution). (s16vc_portfolio_deep_dive.md)
  • Valuation context: $6M post-money on ~$18K ARR = ~333x revenue multiple. Comp: Upflow raised $2.76M seed at an estimated ~$742K revenue with 18 employees and YC W20 backing. (traction_benchmarking.md)

B. Key Arguments & Risks

Why You Should Invest

  • The product is specific to the market, tailored to the existing ecosystem, where almost no competitor has done the same implementation of Poland-specific tools.
  • The problem they focus on is highly relevant in their core market and in other EU countries.
  • The product is focused on a certain, narrow problem and does not try to solve everything at once.

Upside Scenario

They "automate automation" and find a way to connect and maintain the market-specific tools with almost no cost of maintenance. That will let them address tools that are not integrated into their competitors, creating a gap they can fill. And move from the Polish market to the EU with a small to no upcost. (Moving from 50-60M TAM to 1.3-1.4B TAM, giving them the potential to become a unicorn.)

Sources -- Why Invest
  • Polish integrations: Sunbay integrates with Fakturownia, wFirma, inFakt, Systim, Saldeo Smart, Subiekt123. Chaser, Upflow, Gaviti, Tesorio show zero Polish integrations. All 6 Polish tools have public REST APIs rated Low to Moderate difficulty. (competitor_addition.md)
  • Late payment data: Poland 72% affected (EU Payment Observatory 2025, highest in EU). Avg B2B payment period 60+ days (Intrum EPR 2025, n=9,150). 38% of B2B invoices overdue (Atradius 2025, n=210). EU-wide: 52% report late payment problems (2024), up from 42% in 2021.
  • Focused product: Confirmed shipped = reminder sequences, workflows, tracking, interest notes, dashboard. Team of 3 + 1 student, $60K raised. Appropriate scope for stage. (product_analysis.md)
  • TAM upside: Poland TAM = EUR48.7-62.9M (GUS 2024 enterprise counts). EU-27 TAM = EUR1.23-1.48B (Eurostat SBS). (market_sizing_PL.md, market_sizing_EU.md)

Key Risks

  • Scaling outside the current use case will either put them in direct competition with well-established solutions (EU expansion) or at risk of legal liability for AI agents. GDPR compliance is unclear.
  • The team has experience in their jobs, but they are covering responsibilities previously out of scope for them.
  • The product can be replicated relatively easily, especially if the user has an existing CRM.
  • The differentiating point (market-specific tools integration) is shallow -- the tools have APIs, so the barrier to entry for competition is low.
  • The unit economics and other metrics are unclear.

Downside Scenario

Chaser builds an MCP integration platform where users connect their existing accounting software, CRM and banking with zero friction, resulting in a more complete product for $200 instead of EUR120. This makes Sunbay's product obsolete.

Sources -- Risks
  • Funded competitors: Chaser ($4.79M raised, 10K+ users), Upflow ($22.9M), Gaviti ($11.5M), Tesorio ($35.5M). Multi-year head starts.
  • AI regulatory risk: EU AI Act (2024/1689) high-risk classification for legal-adjacent AI. Polish verbal contracts are legally binding. Germany requires two-party consent (s201 StGB). Stanford HAI: legal AI hallucinate in 1/6+ queries.
  • Team gaps: CEO (Filip): zero GTM titles in 9 years. COO/CRO (Dawid): finance ops background, not revenue. CTO (Nikodem): no management experience. All first-time founders.
  • Replicability: All Polish accounting systems have public REST APIs rated Low to Moderate difficulty. "The barrier is knowing which Polish systems to integrate with and doing the work. This is an execution advantage, not a technical moat." (competitor_addition.md)
  • Missing metrics: Current MRR, MoM growth, churn, NRR, CAC, payback period, pipeline, ACV distribution -- none provided. (traction_benchmarking.md)
  • Downside comp: Chaser already has 15+ integrations, payment portal, auto-calls, credit checks, AI payer ratings at $200/mo. (direct_competitor_landscape.md)

Methodology -- TAM Calculation
  • Enterprise scope: 10-249 employees (NACE non-financial economy). Micro (0-9) excluded: too small for dedicated AR tooling. Large (250+) excluded: served by enterprise solutions (Tesorio, HighRadius).
  • ARPC calculation: Midpoint of Sunbay Basic (EUR1,200/yr) and Standard (EUR1,440/yr) = EUR1,320/yr. Custom tier ("contact us") excluded. Blended ARPC depends on customer mix and is not validated against actual revenue.
  • Conservative sectors: B+C+D+E (Industry), F (Construction), H (Transport), M (Professional/scientific/tech), N (Admin & support) -- all have primary B2B invoicing models with 30-60 day credit terms. No B2B/B2C split assumption needed.
  • Extended additions: G46 Wholesale (100% B2B by NACE definition), G45 Motor vehicles (50% B2B -- own estimate), J Info & communication (75% B2B -- driven by J62 IT services), L Real estate (50% B2B -- commercial leasing). G47 Retail excluded (B2C).
  • Data quality ratings: [A] = official government statistics (GUS 2024, Destatis, Eurostat). [B] = survey data (EU Payment Observatory, Intrum, Atradius). [C] = market reports. [D] = own estimates. Conservative TAM uses all [A]-quality sector counts. Extended TAM introduces [B] (Eurostat G-split proportions) and [D] (J/L B2B ratios).
  • Key caveat: No adoption rate applied. This is total addressable market, not serviceable. A realistic SAM would filter for firms with 50+ invoices/month or those reporting late payment issues (72% per EU Payment Observatory). The 10-249 segment is also shrinking (-0.6% YoY); no growth multiplier applied.
  • Sources: GUS Bank Danych Lokalnych (enterprise counts), Eurostat SBS (G-division proportions), Sunbay.io pricing page (ARPC basis).

C6. The Round & Returns

  • Fund is $70M, avg check ~$1-1.4M. The $1.2M round is within terms, but the product does not give a perception of 59x growth potential needed to cover the fund.
  • $1.2M for 20% is too much without proof-of-traction. Requires growth beyond a narrow market and likely a vision change.
  • The angel round ($60K) from Brodziak and Bobrowski is what they need now -- means to find PMF.
  • Successful if they make foundations stronger (less replicable product) or find a market deep enough for 60x growth.

Methodology -- Fund Return Math
  • Ownership: $1.2M / $6M post-money = 20% ownership at entry. With 50% dilution through subsequent rounds: 20% x 0.5 = 10% at exit.
  • 1x fund return: $70M fund / 10% ownership at exit = $700M required exit. Without dilution: $70M / 20% = $350M. The table shows the no-dilution figure; the interactive calculator above models dilution.
  • Market ceiling: Poland TAM EUR49-63M. At 10% market capture (aggressive for seed-stage), revenue = EUR5-6M. At 5-7x revenue multiple, valuation = ~$30-40M. This is ~10x below the $350M needed for 1x fund return without dilution.
  • Check size context: S16VC Fund II ($70M) estimated initial checks at $1-3M. $1.2M is within range but at the lower end. At 20% ownership, S16VC would be lead or sole institutional investor.
  • Fund data sources: eu.vc interview (April 2025, fund size confirmation), PitchBook Fund II, CB Insights, Tracxn (Jan 2026).
  • Data limitation: Individual check sizes are not publicly disclosed. Round sizes are available, but S16VC's specific allocation within rounds is behind PitchBook's paywall. Estimates are triangulated from round sizes, co-investor counts, and fund structure.
  • Source: s16vc_portfolio_deep_dive.md
