My bullet points about Sunbay.io

A. Top Recommendation
Pass
Even though they did find a relevant issue, especially for the Polish market (their main and so far only market), the product itself does not contain any technological know-how, nor do they seem to have any point that will tie clients to them. The product is relatively easily replicable; any tech-savvy user will manage to build the same thing just for their system using APIs in a weekend. [¹] Also, the system at this point is pretty much a complicated reminder feature, with good automations. [²] So far, their main differentiator is focus on the Polish market and Poland-specific tooling, [³] but moving down this road with the current direction, they won't be big enough to cover Fund's Risks. [⁴]

B. Key arguments & Risks

Why you should:
* The product is specific to the market, tailored to the existing ecosystem, where almost no competitor has done the same implementation of Poland-specific tools [¹]
* The problem they focus on is highly relevant in their core market and in other EU countries. [²]
* The product is focused on a certain, narrow problem and does not try to solve everything at once [³]

* Upside scenario: They "automate automation" and find a way to connect and maintain the market-specific tools with almost no cost of maintenance. That will let them address tools that are not integrated into their competitors, creating a gap they can fill. And move from polish market to the EU with a small to no upcost.  (Moving from 50-60M TAM to 1.3-1.4B TAM, giving them the potential to become a unicorn) [⁴]

2. Product
* The product is a solution for a very narrow part of the user flow. They automate invoice payment reminders. But they do not offer the complete payment user flow (including invoice issuance (deck and video say the opposite, but the website has no sign of this feature), payment collection, and client analytics). They are integrated with the tools their ICP on their core market uses. [¹]
* In the pitch video, they claim to have better accuracy for invoice payment monitoring. But I have no data to support this statement. [²]
* In the deck, they have two paths they can scale: Geographic expansion and AI-powered features (Phone call feature using Eleven Labs and Document creation). As for geographic expansion, they risk losing the edge of being ultra-integrated in the local ecosystem, competing with larger players for the same use cases, the same tools, and the same markets. [³] As for AI-powered features, current laws and risk does not seem to make those features a reality in the near future. For example, If the customer will be using the phone call feature to negotiate payment terms, and the person on the other end of the phone will be able to break the guardrails (which is still quite possible since it is easy to gaslight a model into thinking it is on the other side of ethics) and negotiate bad terms for the Sunbay client, who will be responsible? This is both reputational and financial risk, especially considering that the phone agreement is still legally binding in Poland. The same is for the document issuing feature: without a lawyer observing the process, there is no way to guarantee results. [⁴]

AI adoption at 20% (2025), up from 7.7% in 2021. Cloud at 52.7%. (see the details) [²]

4. Competitive Landscape & Differentiation
* As for direct competition in Poland: they are not completely the same as Sunbay, but mostly have similar functionality. Vindicat.pl (claims 20k companies as clients) (won Polish Fintech of the year 2023) is a self-service debt recovery planform with BIG InfoMonitor integration, debt marketplace, e-court filing, legal support. Integrates with the same tools as Sunbay. Comarch ERP Optima (60k users), has automated reminders and sales blocking on overdue clients. Not a standalone product, it requires buying into Comarch's full ecosystem. wFirma.pl has an online accounting platform with basic built-in collections features. 49 PLN/mo for everything. Single-level email reminders, manual SMS [¹]
* As for direct competition outside of Poland: they are Chaser, Upflow, Gaviti and Tesorio. The Chaser is the closest competitor since they implement almost every feature Sunbay has to offer, but Chaser's functionality is way beyond Sunbay's (for only double the price). All of the companies listed above are start-ups raised $4.8-35.5M and are far from the seed round. [²]
* As for indirect competition, there are "Debt recovery agencies" (firmy windykacyjne). PLN 3.7B industry revenue in Poland (2024), 89 firms, 6,801 employees. B2B claims: ~0.9M cases/year, PLN 13.8B nominal value. They operate post-default (30–90+ days overdue) (Sunbay operates pre-default). EU-wide: ~€8.4B market, ~300M accounts placed for collections annually. Major players (Intrum, EOS, Atradius) who mostly serve large corporates, not SMBs. And self automation tools: Zapier: 3M+ users, projected $400M revenue 2025, 8K+ integrations. ~59% US customers. Make.com (Czech-founded): stronger in EU, ~2.5x cheaper than Zapier at comparable volume. n8n: open-source, self-hostable, developer-oriented. Cloud starts at €20/mo. Building an AR reminder pipeline in these tools requires moderate-to-high technical skill: multi-step workflows, external state management (Google Sheets as DB), separate integrations per channel, no native payment matching or collections dashboard. A Zapier Community thread from July 2025 documents a user unable to complete even a basic two-tier reminder system after multiple iterations [³]
* The differentiator factor for Sunbay is its integration in local (Polish) ecosystem. But the point is relatively shallow, since the services they integrate have API, so they can be integrated by others as well. The question is whether the demand is high enough for others to try to integrate into an ecosystem as deep as Sunbay is already [⁴]

7. The Round & Returns
* The Fund is $70M. The average check seems to be $1M+$1.4M. So the 1.2M round seems to be within the common terms, but the product does not give me a perception of the 59X growth potential to cover the fund. According to Tiel in the Zero to One book, in the Venture Capital fund, the risks have to be covered by the potential of the product to outgrow the fund size, which in our case is almost 60x for Sunbay. [¹]
* With the terms of $1.2M for 20% share at this point, that is way too much for the product without actual proof-of-traction. That will require them not only to grow a lot within a narrow market, but also probably change their vision on the future of the company and the scale of the problem they are solving. [²]
* The Angel round ($60K) they raised from Mr Brodziak and Mr Bobrowski seems more like what they need right now. These are the means they can use to figure out PMF and the data they need to convince their product can stand and survive. [³]
* This will be a successful investment for the fund if they find a way to either make their foundations stronger (product less relicatable) or a market deep enough to fulfil the 60x growth. [⁴]
